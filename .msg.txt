fix(dream): reject a set-priority that would not change the score

The first approved plan committed successfully and changed nothing:
before and after revision were identical, the score stayed 50, and the receipt
said committed. The user spent six debugging rounds and a decision on a no-op.

Cause: parsePriority discards the model's score and recomputes it with rescore().
For a record whose attributes already produce 50, rescore() returns 50, so the
action writes back the value it read. Under the current design set-priority can
never change anything — it is a deterministic function of the same inputs.

Rejecting the no-op is the honest fix available without redesigning the action:
the model gets the reason back through the correction round and can either
propose something that matters or submit no change. A silent no-op is worse than
a rejection because it looks like work happened.

The deeper question — whether set-priority should exist at all if the model
cannot move the score — is recorded as a design note rather than decided here.
